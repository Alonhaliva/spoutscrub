The problem

The lid is the hard part.

The inside of a bottle is easy: a brush and some soap. The lid is different. The straw, the spout and the small gaps around them collect buildup, and reaching it usually means taking the lid apart. Meanwhile an open straw rides in bags and cup holders all day.

In a small survey we ran with bottle owners in early 2026, more than half named part of the lid (the seal, the crevices or the spout) as the hardest thing to clean.Our own survey, January–February 2026.
How it works

It cleans every time you drink.

No extra step. The cleaning happens in the move you already make.

STEP 1

Keep it on

The cover sits on the straw tip between sips, in your bag, in the car, on your desk.

STEP 2

Pull it off

On the way off, the soft ribs inside wipe the straw tip all the way around.

STEP 3

Sip, then put it back

Press it back on when you're done. Rinse the cover itself when you wash your bottle.

INSIDE THE COVER

The ribs wipe on the way off.

This animated cutaway removes the front of the cover. The thin ribs on the inside wall touch the straw all the way around, so pulling the cover off wipes the tip.

ALSO A SCRUBBER

Deep-clean a spout in seconds.

On spout lids that already close, use it as a scrubber: slide it over the spout and move it up and down a few times, then rinse.

Which lids

Designed first for straw lids.

A straw tip is open all day, so that's where a cover matters most. Fit testing on each of these starts with the first prototypes.

Straw tumblers

Like the Stanley Quencher. Used as a cover.

Flip straw lids

Like Hydro Flask's Flex Straw lid. Used as a cover.

Push-button spout lids

Like the Owala FreeSip. These close on their own, so here it's a scrubber.
